The strongest one: tests/reference.py rewrites every example the slow, obvious way (plain loops over cells, no shared code), and the engine must agree with it step by step.

Highlighted rows are the ones that matter most for the professor's checklist. The reference implementations are the key idea: two completely different implementations agreeing on hundreds of random grids is much stronger evidence than any single hand-picked example. The whole suite runs in about 12 to 15 seconds with: python -m pytest.
