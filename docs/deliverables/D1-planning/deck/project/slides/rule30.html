The hard case

Why Rule 30 is the hardest case

The Rule 30 Prize asks three open questions about its centre column:

  1. Does it ever become periodic?
  2. Are 0s and 1s equally frequent in the long run?
  3. Can cell n be computed faster than running n steps?

Question 3 is the same as our "answer without running" idea, and it is still an open problem.

Rule 30 is a tiny rule whose centre column looks random enough to have been used as a random number generator. Wolfram's prize offers money for any of these three problems; as far as we know they are still open. For us this means we will not promise a model that predicts Rule 30 far ahead. Instead we measure where prediction breaks and explain why. The table is our own: on a finite ring Rule 30 must eventually repeat, but how long that takes jumps around unpredictably.
